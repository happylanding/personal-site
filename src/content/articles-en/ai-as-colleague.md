> We used to treat AI as an upgraded search box. Now it feels more like a colleague whose abilities keep growing. This article isn't about whether AI will replace humans — it's about how our way of working gets restructured once AI becomes a colleague.

---

## 1. From "Tool" to "Collaborator"

A traditional tool is **deterministic**: you input something, it outputs something. AI, by contrast, is **generative**: you give it a goal, and it gives you "a possible answer."

The difference seems subtle, but it changes the nature of collaboration:

- With a calculator, you figure out the problem and it computes the answer;
- With AI, you figure out what you want, it produces a first draft, and then you judge and polish.

So the workflow shifts from "I execute → I check" to "I direct → AI executes → I review → we iterate." **The ability to review and judge is becoming the new core competency.**

## 2. The New Division of Labor: What People Do, What AI Does

Based on my own hands-on experience, a fairly clear division has emerged:

| Step | Who Leads |
|------|-----------|
| Define the problem / set the goal | Human (AI isn't good at asking "what do you want") |
| Information retrieval / research | AI (fast, thorough, cross-language) |
| First drafts / proposals | AI (efficient, but needs oversight) |
| Judgment / prioritization | Human (AI lacks values and context) |
| Execution / accountability | Human (AI has no "responsibility") |

The core of this division: **AI amplifies "output efficiency"; humans provide "direction and responsibility."** Whoever has stronger judgment holds the initiative in the collaboration.

## 3. How Ordinary People Can Adapt

Instead of worrying about being "replaced," actively do three things:

1. **Put AI to work**: start with the most repetitive tasks you do every day — let AI help with weekly reports, organizing, drafting proposals — and build a feel for it first.
2. **Practice judgment**: when AI gives you ten options, you should be able to say which is better and why. Judgment comes from professional depth and from constant comparison.
3. **Keep asking good questions**: people who ask well get ten times more value from AI. A good question = a clear goal + concrete constraints.

## 4. Closing Thoughts

Technology has never "replaced" humans — it only keeps changing what kind of person is more valuable. When AI becomes a colleague, what's truly scarce isn't people who can write code, but people who can **define problems, hold the direction, and take responsibility for results.**

May we all find our own niche in the collaboration between humans and machines.
