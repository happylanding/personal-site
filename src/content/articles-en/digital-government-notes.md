> As part of the digital economy, digital government has been getting closer to ordinary people's lives in recent years. Behind phrases like "one-stop online services" and "at most one visit" lies a complete set of governance logic. Let me unpack it from a practitioner's perspective.

---

## 1. What "One-Stop Online Services" Actually Changed

On the surface, it moves government services online; in essence, it changes **how information flows**:

- Before: citizens ran back and forth between departments, submitting materials over and over
- Now: data flows between systems, and citizens only submit once

The key isn't the "online form" — it's the **process reengineering** behind "let data run more, let citizens run less." Wherever process reengineering falls short, "online services" just moves the offline hassle onto the web.

## 2. Data as a Factor: The Heart of Digital Government

The most core asset of digital government isn't servers — it's **data**:

- Cross-department data sharing makes "proving I am me" a thing of the past
- Data comparison and verification move approvals from "human judgment" to "system judgment"
- Data aggregation and analysis move policy-making from "experience-driven" to "data-driven"

This also explains why data security and privacy protection matter so much — **the more valuable the data, the greater the responsibility to protect it.**

## 3. Three Levels of Digital Government

1. **Informatization**: moving offline processes online (One-Stop 1.0)
2. **Digitalization**: connecting data and reengineering processes (One-Stop 2.0)
3. **Intelligence**: using AI to support decisions and services (3.0, happening now)

Most regions are still between 1.0 and 2.0, and intelligence has just begun — this is both a challenge and a huge industry opportunity.

## 4. Implications for the Industry

The experience of digital government building is equally relevant to **other fields of industrial digitalization**: data connectivity is the prerequisite, process reengineering is the key, and user experience is the destination. For to-G and to-B digital projects, the order should always be: first clarify the business logic, then talk about the technical solution.

## 5. Conclusion

Digital government is not just "government in the cloud" — it's an upgrade of the entire society's digital governance capability. Understanding its logic means understanding one of the key threads of industrial digitalization.
