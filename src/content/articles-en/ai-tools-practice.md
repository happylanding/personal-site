> The value of a tool isn't in "having it" but in "using it." This article documents 5 real scenarios where I've been using AI productivity tools frequently, and the pitfalls I hit in each one.

---

## 1. Long Documents: Let AI Build the Skeleton, Then Fill in the Flesh

Writing an industry analysis report, I used to stare at a blank document. Now my process is:

1. Hand the topic, target readers, and core points to AI, and have it generate an **outline**
2. I adjust the outline (this is the most important judgment step)
3. Have AI expand section by section, and I revise each section while adding first-hand information

**Result**: writing time roughly halved, and the structure is clearer than before. **Pitfall**: AI's expansions tend to be "correct but hollow" — you need to inject real cases and data by hand.

## 2. Information Organization: "Structuring" Meeting Minutes

After a 1-hour meeting, the transcription is often long and messy. Process it with AI:

- Extract **decisions** (who, when, what gets done)
- Summarize the **to-do list** (owner + deadline)
- Capture **disagreements** (which issues are unresolved)

**Result**: minutes that took 20 minutes to clean up now produce a first draft in 3 minutes. **Pitfall**: AI may mix up attendees' names — always double-check key people and numbers manually.

## 3. Writing Assistance: A "First-Draft Accelerator" for Bilingual Content

My personal site is bilingual, and every article needs an English version. My approach now:

- After writing the Chinese draft, let AI produce a translation draft
- I then polish it manually, checking that **tone and terminology** sound natural
- Maintain a glossary separately to keep translations consistent across the whole site

**Result**: English content production is dramatically faster. **Pitfall**: sentences with heavy literal-translation traces need rewriting, especially cultural references and idioms.

## 4. Knowledge Q&A: AI as a "Personal Advisor"

When facing an unfamiliar domain (a new policy, a new concept), I habitually ask AI for an **overview + keywords** first, then go verify against authoritative sources.

**Result**: lowers the barrier to entering a new field. **Pitfall**: AI is error-prone on time-sensitive information — always trace facts back to sources.

## 5. Code Assistance: Scaffolding for "Non-Programmers"

I'm not a programmer myself, but the site rebuild required code changes. AI helped me:

- Explain the logic of existing code
- Generate small modification proposals
- Pinpoint the cause of build errors

**Result**: someone with a "business background" like me can independently maintain the site. **Pitfall**: always verify AI-generated code in small steps — never "go big bang" straight to production.

## 6. Summary: The Right Way to Use AI Productivity Tools

| Scenario | AI Handles | You Must Handle |
|----------|-----------|-----------------|
| Writing reports | Outline + first draft | Judgment + first-hand info |
| Meeting minutes | Structured cleanup | Names + numbers verification |
| Bilingual writing | Translation draft | Tone + terminology |
| Knowledge Q&A | Overview + keywords | Trace + verification |
| Code assistance | Explain + generate | Small-step verification + accountability |

**In one sentence**: AI is an amplifier, not a stand-in. Use it for "production," and keep "judgment" firmly in your own hands.
