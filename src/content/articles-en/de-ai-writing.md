## 1. First, Know What "AI-ism" Looks Like

The AI feel isn't a grammar error — it's a **recognizable set of clichés**, usually built from three ingredients:

1. **Template connectors**: *First of all / Moreover / Furthermore / In conclusion / It is worth noting*
2. **Hollow buzzwords**: *leverage / synergy / ecosystem / paradigm / cutting-edge / future-proof*
3. **Redundant openers**: *In today's fast-paced world / With the rapid development of ... / In an era of ...*

These words pad the word count without adding information. **What actually carries meaning is facts, numbers, examples, and your own stance** — not the "correct but empty" filler.

Below are three categories of fixes.

---

## 2. Template Connectors: Delete Half, Keep One

AI loves to chain paragraphs with *First / Second / Finally*. The problem isn't the connectors themselves — it's that **every paragraph uses one, in every article**, so structure instantly reads as formula.

| Machine-speak | Human | Note |
|---------------|-------|------|
| First of all | Just start | Say the thing directly |
| Moreover | Also / And | Lighter connector |
| Furthermore | Also | One is enough |
| In conclusion | So / Bottom line | Skip the drumroll |
| It is worth noting | Worth noting / delete | Less emphasis is stronger |
| Needless to say | Delete | Over-assertion rings hollow |

**Rule of thumb**: keep at most one connector per article. The rest of your paragraphs should just start with content — readers don't need a narrator announcing what's next.

---

## 3. Hollow Buzzwords: Swap for Specific Action

Words like *leverage / synergy / ecosystem* aren't bad by nature — but overuse turned them into filler. The test is simple: **if the sentence still works after swapping the word for plain language, the word was padding all along.**

| Machine-speak | Human |
|---------------|-------|
| Leverage | Use / take advantage of |
| Synergy | Working together / combined effect |
| Ecosystem | Community / environment (be specific) |
| Paradigm | Model / way of doing things |
| Cutting-edge | Newest / leading |
| Future-proof | Built to last |
| Seamless | Smooth |

**Rule of thumb**: before writing, ask "what am I actually trying to say?", then use the most concrete verb and noun. Compare *"the platform empowers developers"* with *"the platform cut developers' deploy time from 2 hours to 10 minutes"* — the second has numbers, a subject, and an outcome. That's human writing.

---

## 4. Redundant Openers: Start With the Point

*"In today's fast-paced world"* and *"With the rapid development of technology"* are practically the default opening lines of AI articles. Their problem: **zero information, and they slow the pace.**

| Machine-speak | Human |
|---------------|-------|
| In today's fast-paced world | Just start |
| With the rapid development of ... | Just start |
| In an era of ... | Just start |
| Let's embark on / Let's dive into | Let's / delete |

**Rule of thumb**: open with your most informative sentence. Readers came for content, not a backdrop.

---

## 5. Level Up: Three Things That Give Writing "Bones"

Deleting clichés only gets you a passing grade. To make writing genuinely human, add three things:

1. **Specific details**: add numbers, dates, and scenes. Don't say "efficiency improved" — say "from 3 hours to 40 minutes."
2. **A personal voice**: use "I" and "you", share your own experience and judgment. AI has no lived experience — your story is what no one else can copy.
3. **Imperfect phrasing**: allow short sentences, colloquialisms, even a pause. Too much "smoothness" is itself a tell.
